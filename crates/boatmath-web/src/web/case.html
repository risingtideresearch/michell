In waves

Breaking wave to reach the peak

Quasi-static: the wave is frozen and the platform floats on its surface as on still water so shaped — balanced at each heel in sinkage and free trim — without the wave's orbital pressures or any dynamics. Beam seas run the crests along the platform, quartering seas at 45° to it; for each, the crest is set at eight places along a wavelength and under each hull, and the worst is the crest needing least energy to heel from where the platform rests on it to its peak arm (a crest that pushes it back past upright is its mirror heeling the other way, and is left out). Energy to peak is the weight times the area under the curve from the resting heel to the peak.

The breaking-wave height is a rough estimate, not a criterion: a crest breaking at H/λ ≈ 1/7 strikes at the wave's celerity c = √(gλ/2π), its water above the mean over the hull's length carrying ½ρ(Hλ/2π)·L·c²; if a fraction η of that goes into roll, the height whose crest supplies the energy to peak is H = [8π²E / (49 η ρ g L)]⅓. There is no calibrated η: it goes as η−⅓, so a factor of ten in η is 2.2× in H. Calibrate it against a capsize you know of.
